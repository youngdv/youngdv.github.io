VDOT MRP · Field App

A field inspection app built for glare, rain, and gloves

I turned an 80-page technical manual into a step-by-step, high-contrast mobile app. Inspectors no longer carry binders or paper forms, and the app checks required fields as they go, so nothing gets skipped before they leave the highway.

01
The problem

Safety-critical work, done on paper

Field inspectors rate road assets on highway shoulders, in glare, rain and traffic. They had to follow an 80-page manual and strict audit rules using paper forms, which slowed them down and made it easy to miss a step.

As Lead UX Designer, I owned the move from that manual process to a mobile product, designing in two-week sprints alongside engineering.

02
Design decisions

Every choice answers a field condition

High-contrast UI

Screens wash out in sun and rain, so every view is built for maximum legibility under glare.

Visual perception

Criteria on screen

Inspectors had to remember 80+ pages of passing rules. The rating screen now shows the exact criteria.

Recognition over recall

Oversized targets

Small buttons cause miss-taps in heavy gloves. Every primary action is a large, full-width target.

Fitts's Law

Stop & check

In a rush it's easy to skip one asset among hundreds. The app flags missing required inputs before an inspector can move on.

Forcing function
03
Validation

What the usability study showed

I ran an unmoderated Maze study with 8 participants recruited via Prolific. On their own phones, they found an asset on the map, rated it against IMCS-626 criteria, and finished the documentation. With 8 people, I treated the results as direction, not proof.

Rate the asset75%
Finish documentation50%
Find the asset on the map38%

Task success rate per mission

What worked

Rating, the most safety-critical flow, had the highest success. Participants rated the on-screen criteria 4.9 / 7 for confidence and the Record button 5.2 / 7 for being easy to tap.

What needed work

Finding an asset on the map was the hardest step, with about half of all taps landing as misclicks. Documentation came next.

"Too many buttons to get to the requirements section"

"Bundle records and upload them together"

04
V2 · In testing

Designing V2 from the data

The study pointed at two flows. I started where success was lowest.

Finding an asset: 38% success

V1 used small, unlabeled pins that were easy to miss with a thumb. V2 adds search by route, mile marker or asset, labeled pins with 44px targets, a Near me shortcut, and a List view for anyone who'd rather not use the map. Tapping a pin opens a card with distance and a clear next step.

Documentation: 50% success

Participants asked to bundle records instead of sending them one by one. V2 collects the day's records on one screen to upload together. A record missing a required photo is flagged and held back, so batch upload never skips a required step.

05
Design system

One system, two products

The field app and the management dashboard share one design system, so a status, a color or a button means the same thing on a phone as on a desktop. Tokens are sized per context: 56px controls for gloved hands in the field, 35px for dense desk work.

Color

Navy and orange carry the brand. Every text pairing passes WCAG 2.1 AA (4.5:1), most pass 7:1, for reading in direct sun.
