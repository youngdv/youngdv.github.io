Pointly · Fintech mobile app

Turning everyday credit card points into the subscriptions people already pay for

I designed Pointly, a mobile app that tracks credit card reward points and lets users redeem them for subscriptions like Netflix and Spotify, with full manual control over every redemption.

01
The problem

The problem was trust, not just UI

Reward points are valuable, but most people let them expire or never figure out how to use them. Existing redemption flows are buried, confusing and feel risky.

The hard part was trust: people don't want their points spent automatically without their say-so. Any solution that removed control, even to make things easier, would fail.

02
Research

What the survey revealed

A needs-validation survey confirmed the problem and surfaced the key constraint: users reject automatic redemption by default.

Users want to stay in control: approve each redemption, see their progress, and be able to pause or turn it off. These findings became the core design requirements.

04
Design

From a working flow to explicit control

The Figma prototype covered the full journey: connecting a card, viewing point balances, understanding progress toward a subscription, and completing a manual redemption through the "Use Points" flow.

Testing confirmed the core flow worked, so v2 wasn't about fixing broken flows. Guided by the misclick data, one piece of qualitative feedback and my own heuristic review, I made the trust-and-control model explicit, added clear coverage status and enriched the subscription view.

05
Validation

Usability study

4 participants completed three goal-based tasks on their own phones through Maze (unmoderated): understanding the value and progress concept, completing the "Use Points" redemption, and finding the pause controls.

4 / 4understood the value and progress model
4 / 4completed all three tasks
4.25 / 5average likelihood to use

Every participant paused the subscription, but the 31% misclick rate and the paths told a subtler story: some people looped between the rewards view and the pause screen before finding it. That signal informed making the pause action more discoverable in v2.

"I would have liked some more options for things to do with my points." — one participant

With one open comment across four participants, I treat this as a single signal, not a trend. The study validates first-pass usability and comprehension. It does not establish market demand or long-term behavior.

06
Next

Where Pointly is headed

The prototype showed the concept is learnable and the manual-control model resonates. Pointly is moving toward technical feasibility and development.
