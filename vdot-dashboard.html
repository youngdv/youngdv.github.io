I designed a real-time data hub that connects field inspections to office operations. I replaced a "black box" reporting system with a transparent platform where staff and contractors can instantly verify and act on the data captured by inspectors.

The Project at a Glance

The User, The Challenge, My Role

The User

VDOT operational staff and maintenance contractors.

The Challenge

Field data was historically trapped in paper forms or delayed spreadsheets. Users needed to see what was happening on the roads now to stay compliant with state maintenance standards.

My Role

Lead UX Designer. I designed the web experience to ensure high-density field data was easy to filter, verify, and transform into maintenance work orders.

Part of a connected system

This platform is the office-side of the same system as the Field Inspection App. The design decisions on each side were made in relation to the other. They're not two separate products.

Design Strategy

Designing Without a Research Budget

In a high-velocity development cycle, I used Predictive Design to de-risk the platform. I treated the VDOT Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) as the "Primary User," ensuring that the interface mirrored the legal and operational logic the staff already uses.

How I De-Risked the Design (The HCI Strategy)

Heuristic Evaluation

I audited the platform for Visibility of System Status. I ensured that when an inspector posts from the app, the web user sees a clear status indicator, reducing the uncertainty of "did they finish that route yet?"

Operational Mapping

The website is organized to mirror the IMCS-626 (Asset Condition) and IMCS-627 (Failing Assets) forms. I ensured that the table headers and data fields on-screen perfectly match the legal paperwork users are required to track.

Predictive Search & Filtering

Knowing that users often hunt for specific "Sample Sites" or "Asset IDs" during audits, I built a robust filtering system that allows a user to locate a single failing guardrail out of thousands of records in two clicks.

The Behavioral Science Formula

How Psychology Made Field Data Useful

Skim the bold headers for the 30-second version, or read the Science column to see the depth behind each choice.

The User Challenge My Design Solution The Science (The "DNA")
Data Fragmentation: Inspection records were historically scattered across physical folders and fragmented files. Centralized Data Tables: I designed a "Master View" that pulls all field posts into one searchable grid. Chunking: By grouping related data points (Date, Asset ID, Status) into a single row, I reduced the mental effort required to scan for specific information.
Trust Gaps: Office staff often wonder if a "Failing" rating in the field was subjective or accurate. Direct Photo Integration: I placed field-captured photos directly alongside the rating data in a split-screen view. Visual Proof: Humans process images 60,000x faster than text. Seeing the "why" behind a "Fail" rating builds instant trust and speeds up the repair approval process.
Mental Fatigue: Reviewing hundreds of "Passing" ratings to find the few critical "Failures" is exhausting. High-Signal Status Badges: I used high-contrast "Safety Red" badges for failures and "Neutral Gray" for routine passing items. Signal Detection Theory: I optimized the UI so the "Signal" (the problems) stands out against the "Noise" (the routine data), preventing "alarm fatigue."
From the Shoulder to the Screen

Closing the Loop

The web platform acts as the "Receiver" for the VDOT Mobile App. I designed the interaction to be a seamless feedback loop:

Macro-Level Overview

Users see a statewide or regional map showing the status of all current inspection cycles.

The "Drill-Down" Flow

Clicking a region opens a list of Sample Sites. Clicking a site opens the specific IMCS-626/627 data captured by the mobile app.

Photo-Backed Auditing

For any failing asset, the user can instantly open the high-resolution photo taken on the highway shoulder for immediate verification.

Validation

Validation: Approval on the First Pass

We are currently in the development phase. When these designs were presented to VDOT stakeholders and contractors, they were approved immediately with zero requests for changes.

In a project with no research budget, this "No Feedback" result is the ultimate validation of my HCI approach. It proves that by mirroring the SOP logic and using established behavioral principles, I created a tool that felt so "correct" it required no additional explanation or iteration.

The Outcome

The Final Word

The VDOT MRP Web Platform proves that transparency is the key to successful operations. By using Behavioral Science to organize field data, I built a platform that allows VDOT staff and contractors to stop hunting for information and start acting on it.

  • Real-time visibility: field results available the moment an inspector submits
  • Evidence-based accountability: every failing asset backed by GPS-tagged photos
  • Streamlined audit trails: federal compliance automated as a byproduct of the workflow
