Overview

Building a web presence from scratch

McKenzie Sherman is the founder of Studio88 Keys, a private piano studio serving kids and adults across the DC, Maryland, and Virginia area. She had been growing her studio entirely through word of mouth, but needed a professional web presence to attract new students, communicate her teaching philosophy, and let parents book lessons without back-and-forth emails.

I designed the site in Figma and built it in Framer — a complete 7-page website that positions Studio88 as approachable, trustworthy, and easy to engage with.

The Challenge

Word of mouth only goes so far

McKenzie had no website — all communication happened through email, social media DMs, and referrals. This created several friction points: prospective families couldn't learn about the studio on their own time, there was no clear way to see pricing or policies before reaching out, and McKenzie was spending time answering the same introductory questions repeatedly.

She needed a site that could do the heavy lifting of building trust and answering FAQs so she could focus on teaching.

My Process

Design first, then build

I started in Figma, working through the information architecture, page layouts, and visual direction before moving into production. This included mapping the site's navigation structure across all 7 pages, exploring typography pairings and color palette options, and building component-level layouts for reusable sections — nav, footer, CTA blocks, and closure cards. McKenzie reviewed and approved the visual direction before I committed to code.

Once the designs were approved, I moved into Framer to build the live site — translating Figma comps into responsive, production-ready pages with working interactions, a contact form, and CMS-ready content sections.

Wireframe to hi-fi

Design

Every decision had a reason

Warm, inviting color palette

Cream background with burnt orange and forest green accents reflects the studio's personality — playful but grounded. The palette avoids the cold, corporate feel of many service websites and creates a sense of warmth that mirrors McKenzie's teaching style.

Expressive typography

The hero section uses a serif italic for key words to inject personality and movement into the headline. This typographic contrast gives the brand a memorable identity without relying on heavy illustration or stock imagery.

Trust-building through transparency

Rather than hiding pricing and policies behind a contact form, I designed dedicated Tuition and Policy pages that lay everything out upfront — lesson durations, monthly rates, cancellation rules, holiday schedules. Parents can make informed decisions before ever reaching out, which filters for serious inquiries.

Storytelling through structure

The About section uses a numbered timeline to walk visitors through the studio's origin story. This narrative approach builds emotional connection and positions McKenzie as someone who grew organically, not a faceless business.

Founder-forward design

The "Meet the Founder" section gives McKenzie a full portrait, bio, and personal quote. For a service where parents are trusting someone with their child, seeing and connecting with the actual instructor is critical to conversion.

Social proof through a real gallery

The "Inside the Studio" gallery showcases real recital photos, students with certificates, and candid teaching moments. This is more persuasive than any testimonial block — it shows a thriving, active community.

Clear conversion path

Every page includes a persistent "Book a Lesson" CTA in the navigation. The Contact page pairs a simple form with studio hours, location, and email so visitors have multiple ways to engage. No dead ends.

Closure calendar

The "Upcoming Closures" section with dated cards shows operational maturity and helps set expectations for prospective and current families — a small detail that reduces support questions.

The Outcome

A studio that speaks for itself

McKenzie was thrilled with the final product. The site gave her studio a professional, polished identity for the first time and immediately reduced the volume of repetitive inquiry questions she was fielding.

She now has a single link to share with prospective families that answers their questions, builds trust, and drives them toward booking — all before she ever picks up the phone.
