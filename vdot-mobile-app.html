The Project at a Glance

The User, The Challenge, My Role

The User

Field inspectors standing on highway shoulders in rain, heat, and high-speed traffic.

The Challenge

Assessing dozens of assets (Pavement, Signs, Guardrails) while following strict federal audit rules, on paper forms, in the field.

My Role

Lead UX Designer. I owned the full transition from the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to the final mobile interface.

Design Strategy

Designing Without a Research Budget

This was a fast-moving project with limited field time and no budget for traditional user studies. I leaned on my Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) background to de-risk the design and ensure high-stakes government requirements were met without slowing down delivery.

How I De-Risked the Design (Predictive Engineering)

Instead of guessing, I used established behavioral principles to audit the interface:

SOP as the "Primary User"

I treated the mandatory forms (IMCS-626/627) as the source of truth. I built Forcing Functions into the UI to prevent the exact human errors the manual warned against.

Predictive Task Mapping

Based on the 80-page SOP, I designed a "Stepped Flow," breaking the task into smaller, linear stages. This prevents Cognitive Overload by ensuring the inspector only focuses on one asset at a time.

Heuristic Evaluation

I performed a professional audit of every screen to ensure Error Prevention. I made the app feel like a natural extension of the VDOT workflow, even without a formal field study.

The Behavioral Science Formula

How Psychology Drove Every Design Decision

The Field Challenge
My Design Solution
The Science (The "DNA")
Environmental Glare: Screens wash out in direct sunlight or heavy rain.
Ultra-High Contrast UI: Deep-sea blue background with vibrant "Safety Green/Red" buttons.
Visual Perception: Under physical stress and glare, the eye struggles with subtle shades. I optimized for maximum legibility under load.
Complex Rules: Inspectors had to remember 80+ pages of "Passing" rules.
Criteria-at-a-Glance: I placed the specific IMCS-626 passing/failing rules directly on the rating screen.
Recognition over Recall: It is easier for the brain to recognize a rule on a screen than to recall it from memory while distracted by traffic.
Physical Limits: Inspectors wear heavy gloves. Small buttons lead to "miss-taps."
Oversized Hit Zones: Every primary action button (Pass/Fail) is a large, full-width target.
Fitts's Law: The time to hit a target is a function of its size. Large buttons reduce errors for users with limited dexterity.
Slip-Errors: In a rush, it is easy to forget to rate one asset among hundreds.
The "Stop & Check": The app flags missing required inputs (Form IMCS-627) before an inspector can advance.
Forcing Functions: By creating a small, intentional "speed bump," I prevented hours of manual data correction work later.
From Manual to Mobile

Turning the Manual (SOP) into a Product

The original VDOT SOP was a dense technical document. I broke it down into a Stepped Flow that mirrors the inspector's physical walk-around:

Guided Rating (IMCS-626)

By using a Stepped Flow, I break the inspection into logical "chunks" (Pavement → Drainage → Traffic Services). This follows Miller's Law, keeping the number of items a user has to remember at any one time to a minimum.

Instant Failing Reports (IMCS-627)

If an asset fails, the app immediately triggers a request for a photo and a maintenance note. No offline reconciliation needed.

Validation

Validation & Beta Testing

We conducted a Beta Test with real inspectors to ensure technical stability. While the field provided no direct feedback, the result was a success: "No news is good news."

Because I had already applied HCI principles to the DNA of the app, the inspectors were able to pick up the tool and use it immediately with zero training. The lack of negative feedback validated that the predictive design was intuitive enough to handle the complexity of the job.

The Outcome

The Final Word

This project proves that you don't need a massive research budget to create a successful product. By understanding how the human brain processes information under stress, I delivered a tool that is now used across Virginia to manage millions of dollars in road repairs safely and accurately.

  • 100% digital: eliminated all paper forms and manual re-entry
  • Zero data gaps: the "Stop & Check" catches skipped fields before inspectors leave the site
  • Enhanced safety: lower mental tax lets inspectors stay aware of their surroundings
