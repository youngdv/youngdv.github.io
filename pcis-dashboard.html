Designing a Multi-Stakeholder Port Logistics Platform

Service design and role-based interfaces for terminal operators, carriers, and cargo owners — including safety-critical hazardous cargo workflows.

The Challenge

The Ecosystem

Port logistics is not a single-user problem. It is an ecosystem of competing priorities: terminal operators need efficient throughput, carriers need to minimize wait time, and beneficial cargo owners need visibility into their specific shipments. Add customs, safety inspectors, and IT systems, and you have 6+ stakeholders interacting through fragmented communication channels with no shared visibility.

PCIS was designed to be the connective tissue — a unified platform that gives each stakeholder a tailored view of shared operational data.

Research

Service Blueprint

Before designing any screens, I mapped the entire service ecosystem. The service blueprint captures:

  • Frontstage actions: What each stakeholder does and sees
  • Backstage processes: System integrations, data flows, and manual processes that support the frontend
  • Support processes: IT infrastructure, compliance checks, and inter-organizational communication
  • Pain points: Where information breaks down between stakeholders, causing delays and manual workarounds

[PLACEHOLDER: Full service blueprint showing frontstage/backstage/support layers across terminal operator, carrier, and BCO journeys — wide, detailed diagram. Gray/blue color scheme with red markers at pain points]

The blueprint revealed that the highest-friction points were not within any single role's workflow — they were at the handoff points between stakeholders. These handoffs became my primary design targets.

User Research

Persona Deep Dives — Conflicting Needs

Terminal Operator

Primary goal: Maximize throughput and minimize yard congestion

Key workflow: Container availability management, appointment approvals, yard planning

Tension: Wants to batch-process approvals for efficiency, but carriers need individual real-time updates

Transportation Operator (Carrier)

Primary goal: Minimize wait time and maximize daily pickups/deliveries

Key workflow: Appointment scheduling, real-time container status checks, route planning

Tension: Needs instant visibility into availability, but terminals update status in batches

Beneficial Cargo Owner (BCO)

Primary goal: Shipment visibility and partner communication

Key workflow: Tracking container status, communicating with carriers and terminals

Tension: Wants granular shipment-level detail, but system data is organized by container, not shipment

These tensions meant I could not simply design three versions of the same dashboard. Each persona needed a fundamentally different information hierarchy built on shared underlying data.

IA & Design

Information Architecture Across Personas

The core IA challenge was maintaining consistency (same navigation patterns, same visual language, same terminology) while tailoring information priority to each role. I designed a shared navigation shell with role-specific default views:

  • Shared elements: Global navigation, notification system, search, and container detail views
  • Role-specific defaults: Terminal operators land on an approval queue and yard overview. Carriers land on appointment scheduling and container availability. BCOs land on shipment tracking and communication feeds.

This approach meant users who interact across roles experience a consistent system, while their primary workflow is immediately accessible.

Safety Design

The Hazardous Cargo Workflow

Hazardous cargo is the highest-stakes workflow in the port ecosystem. Different regulations (IMDG Code, 49 CFR), different handling requirements, different urgency levels, and different approval chains. A mistake here has safety consequences.

I designed the hazardous cargo workflow with additional safeguards:

  • Visual differentiation: Hazmat containers are flagged with persistent warning indicators across all persona views — not just color coding, but iconography and placement changes that make them impossible to miss even when scanning quickly
  • Mandatory confirmation steps: Approval workflows for hazmat require explicit acknowledgment of cargo class and handling requirements before status changes
  • Cross-stakeholder visibility: When a hazmat container's status changes, all relevant stakeholders receive proactive notifications — not just the next person in the chain
  • Audit trail: Every action on a hazmat container is logged with timestamps and actor identification for compliance
System Design

Cross-Stakeholder Communication Design

One of the most complex design challenges was the communication layer between personas. I designed a unified notification and messaging system that:

  • Surfaces relevant updates in each persona's dashboard without requiring them to check a separate inbox
  • Contextualizes messages with container/shipment data so recipients do not need to cross-reference
  • Supports both automated status notifications and human-written messages in the same thread
