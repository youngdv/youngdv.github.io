Saving Time (and Stress) on the Highway

I replaced a messy paper process with a mobile app that works in the rain, next to 60mph traffic. This is a real-world example of Behavioral Science applied to product design.

Industry
Government / Transportation
Client
Virginia DOT

My Role

Team

The Challenge

The "Pinpoint"

Inspectors aren't sitting at desks. They are on the side of the highway in heat, rain, and glare. They wear gloves. Trucks are flying by at 60mph. They're rating pavement, guardrails, and signage — and every decision feeds into millions of dollars in repair planning.

I had to solve two main problems:

  • Mental Load: It's hard to think clearly when you're in a dangerous environment.
  • Physical Limits: Small buttons don't work with gloves or wet screens.
The Behavioral Formula

How Behavioral Science Drove Every Decision

Skim the bold headers for the 30-second version — or read the Science column to see the depth behind each choice.

The Problem
My Design Fix
The Science (Why it works)
Information Overload
One step at a time. I used a "stepped" flow instead of a big dashboard.
Cognitive Load: When you're stressed, your brain can only handle one choice at a time. I kept the focus on the current task.
Environmental Stress
Big buttons & high contrast. Everything is oversized and bright.
Physical Ergonomics: Gloved hands aren't precise. High glare makes normal colors disappear. I designed for "worst-case" visibility.
Distraction
Hidden history. I hid old data behind a "tap" so it only shows when needed.
Progressive Disclosure: By hiding secondary info, I stopped "visual noise" from distracting the inspector from the primary rating.
Human Error
The "Stop & Check." The app flags missing info before they can move to the next segment.
Forcing Functions: In a rush, it's easy to forget a field. This small "speed bump" prevents hours of correction work later.
Research Methods

How I Proved It Worked

I didn't just guess — I used Human Factors methods:

Task Analysis

I mapped out every single move an inspector makes to ensure the app followed their natural rhythm — what they do, in what order, under what conditions.

Cognitive Walkthroughs

I stepped through the design as if I were in the rain and distracted, to find "bottlenecks" before they became real problems. This is how I caught the dashboard layout failing before we built it.

Beta Testing

We put the app in the hands of real inspectors. This is how I discovered they were skipping fields — a consistent pattern across participants, not individual error. That insight directly led to the "Stop & Check" feature.

The Outcome

The Final Word

This project proves that Behavioral Science is the best tool for "no-budget" research. By understanding how the human brain works under pressure, I built a tool that is now used across Virginia to manage millions of dollars in road repairs.

  • 100% digital — eliminated all paper and manual re-entry
  • Zero data gaps — the "Stop & Check" catches skipped fields before they cause downstream problems
  • Lower cognitive load — inspectors can stay aware of their surroundings instead of wrestling with their screens
  • Part of a connected system — see the Management Dashboard case study for the other half

The team

I was the sole UX designer on a cross-functional Alpha Team at Leidos: a Product Manager, Solutions Architect, Software Engineer, and Engineering Intern.

My work included partnering with the Solutions Architect to understand data model constraints that shaped interface possibilities, working directly with VDOT stakeholders to translate operational requirements into design decisions, and facilitating beta testing sessions with field inspectors to synthesize feedback into iterations.
